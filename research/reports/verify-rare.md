# Verification: verify:rare

Overall reliability: The report is mostly reliable on its sources. D-Fire CC0 and its counts, the model licences (OWLv2, Grounding-DINO, OmDet, Florence-2, YOLOE, YOLO-World, SAM 3), the YOLO26 latencies, the SAHI gains, the AI City 2021 method and score, Porikli's dual foreground, SMIYC counts, Lost and Found's non-commercial licence, the VLM weight sizes and the ByteTrack defaults all check out against primary sources. The MOG2 absorption formula reproduces within 5%. The spec reading is largely correct: the union-of-intervals convention, back-dating, the class-dilution rule, and end = duration for events that run past the end. The main problems are engineering, not citations. The perspective model is wrong: k should be 0.0457, not 0.16, and a linear row scale cannot convert along-road speeds, which skews the stopped_vehicle and congestion thresholds. Florence-2 cannot do yes/no VQA. The zero-FP audit cannot statistically certify the 0.05 FP/h target. The 1080p DIS timing is about 6–9x too high. OmDet's 100 FPS was measured on an A100, not a T4. Path A for road_obstacle is enabled by default, breaking the report's own gating rule. The compute plan ignores decode cost and the harness's shared A+B budget, where an overrun also wipes Part A. The overall architecture is usable, but fix these before implementing.

## Spec / logic errors
- Perspective model (§1.4): the report says k=0.16, but that is the lane-width slope in px per px. The px/m slope is 0.0457, so the formula as written contradicts the 34 and 11 px/m it quotes. More fundamentally, s(y) = k(y−y_h) is valid only for lateral distances. Along-road motion is foreshortened by h/Z, so every m/s threshold (still < 0.3 m/s, jam ≤ 2 m/s, rider > 3.5 m/s, displacement < 0.7 m / 5 s) is miscalibrated for longitudinal motion. A slowly crawling car can be classed as 'still', which inflates stopped_vehicle and congestion. Use a ground-plane homography built from camera.md and lane markings.
- Dilution rule audit (§1.2, §5, §6.3): the report demands λ ≤ 0.05 FP/h and says '0 false positives on every sample video plus 1–2 h external video' shows it. Zero events in T hours only bounds λ < 3/T per hour at 95% (rule of three). A few minutes of samples plus 2 h of external video bounds λ at about 1.5/h, roughly 30x above the target. The audit cannot certify the rule. The T = 2 h test-set length is also an assumption: the spec gives only 'other videos ... several minutes long'.
- Internal inconsistency on road_obstacle: §1.1 sets min_len = 3 s, but path A accepts animals on the carriageway for ≥ 1 s. A 2 s dog crossing would be deleted by the post-processor.
- Internal inconsistency on precision gating: path A (COCO cat/dog/horse/sheep/cow) is 'enabled by default' as 'cheap, precise' without the zero-FP audit the report requires for B+C and fire_smoke. COCO detectors routinely produce small-scale false 'dog', 'horse' or 'cow' boxes on CCTV (bags, motorbikes, pedestrians). By the report's own §1.2 rule, road_obstacle (low p) should only be enabled after the same audit.
- §6.4 and top recommendation 8 list Florence-2-large as a VLM for yes/no verification. Florence-2 has no free-form VQA task, so it can only verify via phrase grounding or open-vocabulary detection.
- §1.5 recipe: 'createBackgroundSubtractorMOG2(500,16,False); alpha_s ≈ 0.007' only gives ~3 s absorption if learningRate=alpha_s is passed to every apply() call. The default auto rate (1/history = 0.002) absorbs in ~10.5–11 s (verified), so the short-term model would be much slower than intended.
- §1.5: 'B_long init = per-pixel median ... over the whole video (offline is allowed)'. Any vehicle stationary for more than ~50% of the sampled frames becomes part of B_long, which disables the ghost and occlusion logic for exactly the long stopped_vehicle events that matter, and for obstacles that stay most of the clip. Use a median over frames where the detector sees no vehicle at that pixel, or a window before the candidate event.
- §10 says 'Part B may not reuse Part A outputs'. The spec's prohibition is narrower: 'reusing Part A output that was computed with future frames'. Causal Part A signals may be shared. This is safe but overstated.
- §1.1: 'count decoded frames for duration ... clamp so validation cannot reject an event'. The harness (run_submission.py) already clamps end to CAP_PROP_FRAME_COUNT/fps. The evaluate.py check (end ≤ GT duration + 0.5 s) is a file-level error that invalidates the whole predictions file, not just one event. Use the harness's duration formula rather than a separate decode count.
- Congestion: jam_d requires ≥ 80% of lanes jammed. The spec says 'across all lanes of a direction', so for directions with 5 or more lanes this is looser than the definition (4 of 5 lanes would count).
- jaywalking: the start is the first sample of the run where d ≥ 0.4 m, but the spec's start is 'pedestrian steps onto road' (d crosses 0) while the end uses d < 0. This introduces a systematic late start of ~0.3 s; back-date to the last 0-crossing. Excluding the whole intersection box to drop police or workers would also remove real diagonal jaywalking through the intersection.
- IoU tolerance table: the L = 5 s 'both ends inward' figure is 0.75 s, not 0.8 s; rounded up, it would fail τ = 0.7 (IoU 0.68).
- §0 says the static engine costs 10–15 ms per processed frame; §8 says 30–50 ms per video-second at 5 fps (6–10 ms per frame). The two figures disagree, and the 1080p DIS figure of 226 ms does not reproduce (25–40 ms measured on the same CPU).
- The §8 compute budget leaves out video decoding and relies on TensorRT-only latencies (YOLO26, OmDet on A100). The budget covers Part A + Part B, and an overrun empties Part A events for that video too (run_submission.py), so the margin claims 'well inside 3x' are optimistic until measured with PyTorch or ONNX Runtime on a T4.


## Missing points
- A metric ground-plane calibration: a homography from camera.md lane geometry, crosswalk stripe spacing, dash lengths and the 3.5 m lane width, instead of a 1-D row scale. Every threshold in metres or m/s for stopped_vehicle, congestion, jaywalking and obstacles depends on it.
- The starter kit is already in the scratchpad (kit/wiut_cv_scripts). The report should have checked its exact mechanics. The harness clamps end to n_frames/fps, drops same-class overlaps keeping the earlier one, and runs Part A before Part B. The shared 3x budget empties both A and B on overrun. evaluate.py tolerates end ≤ duration + 0.5 s, and M = Score_A when the test set has no accidents.
- Video decode cost: about 12 ms per frame for 1080p cv2.read() on the dev CPU. The harness decodes every frame for Part B, so Part A should decode once and share frames across the detector, static engine and fire detector. Consider hardware decode (NVDEC via PyAV or decord) on the T4.
- Deployment realism: TensorRT engines are not portable across GPU and TensorRT versions, and building one on the evaluation box eats a video's budget. Budget with PyTorch FP16 or ONNX Runtime CUDA latencies and ship the YOLOE CLIP and tokenizer dependencies in requirements.txt.
- Determinism: VLM verification must use greedy decoding with fixed seeds. Thresholds near decision boundaries (fire confidence 0.35/0.45, open-vocabulary 0.2) can flip whole segments between runs under fp16 nondeterminism, so add hysteresis margins, as the Code rubric requires reproducible predictions_samples.json.
- The statistical limits of a zero-FP audit (rule of three), and a concrete fallback: raise precision thresholds per class until the observed FP-per-hour upper bound is acceptable, or keep fire_smoke and the static-blob obstacle path off unless the samples or organisers show these events exist.
- A question to the organisers (the report raises one only for congestion). Also ask whether buses at stops count as stopped_vehicle, whether crashed vehicles after an accident get a separate stopped_vehicle label, and whether a stray animal crossing counts as road_obstacle. These definitions drive most of the F1 for these classes.
- Conditional plans for scene variables still unknown (camera.md is not yet available): the resolution (720p vs 1080p vs 4K changes person pixel heights and decode cost), day or night or IR clips, and whether the signal is visible. The report assumes 1080p throughout.
- The README requirement to list every dataset and model licence: COCO (CC BY 4.0, used by the detector weights), D-Fire CC0, Ultralytics AGPL weights, and any external negative traffic video. Also state that FASDD and Lost and Found are unusable or non-commercial.
- Website and EDA hooks specific to these classes: a stop heatmap, a pedestrian foot-point heatmap and congestion timelines are mentioned only briefly. Showing injection-test results and failure cases for rare classes would serve the 'honest failure cases' requirement.
