## Spec / logic errors
- Model loading versus the time budget: the report recommends 'a lazily created module-level singleton', and the pseudo-code loads the model on the first _model() call. The harness executes solution.py via load_solution() at run_submission.py line 146, before the per-video loop and before t0 = time.perf_counter() at line 167. Loading the model eagerly at import time, plus a warm-up forward pass (CUDA context, cuDNN, and a TensorRT engine if used), costs no video's budget. The lazy version charges all of it to the first video's Part A.
- The free-ride claims ignore that the metric merges before it filters. alarm_starts() merges runs less than 2 s apart (evaluate.py lines 269-274), and only then drops alarms whose start is ignored (line 300). So 'A post-contact detector cannot hurt Score_B' is false for secondary collisions. The near-miss window is not free either when a true pre-crash alarm follows within 2 s: that alarm is swallowed and the accident is missed. Both cases were demonstrated with the official code. The report's traps list covers merging with an early false alarm, but not merging with an ignored-start run.
- Contact suppression (30 s, 15 m, u -= 6) directly suppresses secondary or chain collisions within 30 s. It also relies on a contact detector whose false positives would silence real alarms. The toy that 'validates' it (+0.02) uses ground-truth contact times and no false contact detections, so the gain is an upper bound.
- Two-band score (section 4.2): with score = 0.5+0.4999r when the alarm is on and 0.4999r when off, AP ranks frames by alarm state first and r second. Every alarm-on frame outranks every alarm-off frame, including frames held on by release, 3 s gap bridging or the 8 s cap. The 'ranking head r drives AP' separation is therefore not real: alarm-shaping choices directly cost AP, as the toy's hysteresis runs show (AP 0.163→0.145).
- The scale-invariance argument ('the projective part of the homography matters more than getting the absolute scale right') contradicts the report's own metric footprints and thresholds, all of which need correct absolute scale. Calibrate scale carefully from camera.md lane widths and stop-line or crosswalk dimensions, not only the median car length.
- Marginal-value table (section 2.1) says it assumes recall 0.5 and false alarms = 0.5N, but the N=3 and N=5 rows were computed with integer-rounded m = FP = 2. That makes the q* column (0.29, 0.22) look N-dependent when under the stated assumption it is 0.25 for every N. The qualitative conclusions still hold.
- The toy-sim summary 'best threshold always at 0.27-0.36 FA/accident' is overstated. The re-run shows a flat optimum from about 0.3 to 0.8 FA/accident (argmax at 0.59 for 0.3 benign/min). The FA/h equivalents assume the toy's 6 accidents per hour. Setting a budget of both '0.3-0.5 per expected accident' and '1-2 per hour' implicitly assumes about 3-4 accidents per hour of test footage, while the report elsewhere says density is unknown.
- Engineering safety: the blanket try/except in step() combined with torch.use_deterministic_algorithms(True) can silently freeze the score after the first RuntimeError. If the frozen value is 0.5 or above, the result is one very long alarm that cannot match anything and blocks later alarms. The exception path should return a decaying or zero score and log the error, and deterministic mode should use warn_only=True.
- Pseudo-code ByteTrack parameters: track_buffer is rescaled internally by frame_rate/30, so the intended 1.5 s buffer becomes about 0.6 s at stride 2.
- Minor: 'stride 3 is only 15-30% noisier' should be about 30% at the 1.5 s window, per the report's own table. The 'emergency stride increase based on elapsed time' is wall-clock dependent. It conflicts with the rule 'two runs on same machine must give same predictions.json', which the report acknowledges only partly.
- Deliberate deviation from the organizers' tip ('calibrate so 0.5 means probably within 5 s'): the report moves the 0.5 crossing to a match probability of about F1/2 for a 10 s window. This is metric-correct under the code, but it should be stated explicitly as a departure. The alarm head's target is 'accident within W = 10 s', not H = 5 s.


## Missing points
- Single-vehicle and fixed-object crashes: the spec's accident definition includes 'road user and fixed object'. All conflict features are pairwise between tracked road users. Barriers, poles, kerbs and islands from camera.md should be added as static capsules, together with loss-of-control cues such as a sudden heading change toward the road edge.
- ACCIDENT dataset limits for anticipation. Labels for all 2,027 real clips are public, not just 507. But the median pre-impact context is 4.67 s: only 46% of clips have at least 5 s before impact and 9% have at least 10 s. Median fps is 15, and there are no accident-free clips. So it cannot supply negatives, cannot test [s-10, s-5) behaviour for most clips, and needs timing resampled to 25 fps. The Kaggle licence is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and the videos appear to be YouTube-sourced.
- Negative data for false-alarm calibration: a few sample clips (possibly under an hour in total) cannot estimate a '1-2 false alarms per hour' budget reliably, since observing 0 or 1 false alarm is uninformative. Bootstrap intervals and extra accident-free fixed-camera footage (e.g. UA-DETRAC, licence permitting) are needed as a stress test.
- Small-object recall at imgsz=640 on 1080p/4K wide CCTV frames: far-field pedestrians and motorcycles may drop out. Options such as ROI crops, tiling or imgsz 960/1280 need to be weighed against the budget.
- Robustness at night, in rain and under glare: tracking-based TTC degrades there, and a cheap fallback cue (e.g. optical-flow or frame-difference energy near a conflict zone) could help. In ACCIDENT, optical-flow heuristics and zero-shot VLMs were the baselines, and the best of them was Molmo at 0.343.
- Multiple accidents less than 10 s apart (pile-ups): each needs its own alarm with at least a 2 s off-gap, and the earliest-first matching means a single long alarm can match only one of them.
- Part B is skipped entirely if Part A alone passes the deadline (run_submission.py line 184), and then both are emptied. So Part A needs its own internal time guard. The report covers the Part B guard only.
- A CI-style smoke test running the unmodified run_submission.py plus evaluate.py --validate-only on a sample, to catch the silent-exception and frozen-score failure modes, and a check that the risk curve has one entry per frame.
