# Verification: verify:rules

Overall reliability: Mostly reliable. The metric arithmetic is correct throughout: the IoU tolerance table, the k/(k+1) gating penalty, the 0.30–0.64 break-even range, the 120 ms/frame budget, the SG noise and brake-step simulations all reproduce. Of 18 key claims, 15 are confirmed (several with nuances), 2 are partially correct (the DRAC 3.35 attribution to FHWA; the GOST R 51256 dash lengths, where the relevant standard is Uzbekistan's O'z DSt 3419:2019), and 1 is refuted (the cited NGSIM repo uses SG order 3 with a ~2 s window, not order 1 with 1 s). Beyond the key claims, one real technical error: findHomography with a RANSAC threshold of 3.0 in metres. The main logic gaps: the claim that per-class F1 is independent contradicts the report's own cross-class suppression; zero FPs on the samples does not imply q ≈ 0 on a longer, shifted test set; the ≥1.5 s SG window conflicts with the 0.3 s hard-brake gate; rounding after clipping can break end <= duration; 'A may use B' is not costed against the 3x budget; and two labelling assumptions (red queues vs congestion, jams vs stopped_vehicle) are not flagged as questions. The per-class designs match the spec's start/end conventions well, and the design is a sound starting point once these fixes are made.

## Spec / logic errors
- Homography fit (§1.4): cv2.findHomography(img, world, cv2.RANSAC, 3.0) with world points in metres sets a 3 m inlier tolerance, because the threshold is in destination units. RANSAC then removes nothing useful. Use least squares (method 0) on the hand-picked points and check residuals in metres.
- 'A may use B' (§2.11) ignores the interface. detect_events(video_path) receives no risk curve: the harness calls detect_events and separately 'streams every frame through RiskEstimator'. To use B's curve in A, detect_events must build and run its own RiskEstimator over the video, which charges B's cost twice against the shared limit ('Time per video (A + B) at most 3x video duration'). The budget in §1.1 does not account for this, or for B needing its own causal detection pass, since the report itself says A must not feed B.
- Tuning independence claim (§3: 'Per-class F1 does not depend on other classes, so use coordinate descent') contradicts the report's own cross-class suppression. congestion_active gates stopped_vehicle and near_miss, stop_line and red_light are made mutually exclusive, and accident excludes near_miss. Changing congestion thresholds changes stopped_vehicle F1. Tune in dependency order (congestion, then stop_line/red_light, then stopped_vehicle; accident, then near_miss) or jointly.
- Class-gating logic (§0.2): 'zero false positives across all sample minutes' does not establish q ≈ 0. With T_s sample minutes and 0 FPs, the 95% upper bound on the FP rate is about 3/T_s (rule of three), so q on a test set of length T_t can be as large as 1 - exp(-3*T_t/T_s). The spec also warns test videos 'contain events not in the samples', which is distribution shift. q must be scaled by test length and checked on extra negative footage.
- Near-miss evasive gate is inconsistent with the smoothing advice. §1.4 requires SG windows >= 1.5 s for acceleration, and §2.12 requires 'decel >= 3.4 for >= 0.3 s'. Simulated: a real -5 m/s^2 brake lasting 0.5 s peaks at only -2.9 m/s^2 after SG(1.5 s), and a 0.3 s brake at -1.9, so neither ever triggers. Only brakes of about 0.7 s or longer pass. Use a speed-drop criterion (for example Δv >= 2–3 m/s within 1 s) or a shorter window in the near field.
- Post-processing order (§3 steps 7–8): clipping to duration and then rounding to 0.01 s can push end_sec above duration (e.g. duration 123.456 gives 123.46). That violates the spec rule '0 <= start_sec < end_sec <= duration'. Round first, then clip, flooring the end. Also compute duration from decoded frames, since CAP_PROP_FRAME_COUNT can be off.
- Unflagged labelling assumptions that change GT agreement. (a) stopped_vehicle is suppressed inside congestion and for yielding or waiting vehicles, but the spec only excludes vehicles 'in a queue at a signal'. (b) congestion drops normal red-phase queues, but the spec defines congestion as 'traffic at a standstill or crawling across all lanes of a direction', which a long red queue literally meets. Neither assumption is on the organizer question list (Q6 only asks about minimum duration).
- red_light vs stop_line (§2.6(f)/§2.7(f)): the report asserts that a vehicle crossing on red and then stopping before the box is 'stop_line only'. But the spec defines red_light as 'vehicle crosses the stop line while its signal is red' (Start: front crosses stop line), which that vehicle meets, and the spec allows different classes to overlap. Treat this as open (it is asked in Q2) and decide from dev labels or the organizers' answer, not by assertion.
- Weak inference (§0.2): 'Part B ignores frames around near_miss events. That implies near_miss is annotated in the hidden GT'. The ignore rule is generic metric machinery. Every one of the 14 classes is 'official, any may appear in hidden test', so this says nothing about whether near_miss actually occurs in the test set.
- Location assumption: the PDF never says where the camera is. The report builds rules on Uzbek SDA (sign 5.42 right-on-red, clause 41 stop line, sign 3.19) and 'Tashkent-type' frequencies. Tashkent is plausible (WIUT), but these rules must be conditional on camera.md. Labels follow the spec's class definitions, not local law; for example, running a yellow is never red_light.
- Kinematics-valid zone (§1.4) uses a scalar ppm(u,v). Longitudinal braking of vehicles approaching or leaving the camera lies along the image depth axis, where pixels-per-metre is smallest. The zone should use ppm along the travel direction (or the minimum singular value of the Jacobian of H^-1), otherwise the near_miss and accident braking rules run in areas where noise exceeds the 3.4 m/s^2 threshold.
- ByteTrack track_buffer (§1.5): Ultralytics sets max_frames_lost = track_buffer in tracker updates, with no frame-rate scaling. With the report's own stride-2 decoding, 75 frames means about 6 s, not 3 s, which raises ID-switch risk for the reversal and U-turn rules.
- road_obstacle branch B (§2.13): LT = median of frames over 120 s absorbs any obstacle present for more than about 60 s of that window. For long-lasting debris the blob fades and the 'removed' end fires too early. LT must be frozen from frames before the obstacle appears, both to place the end correctly and to measure its duration.
- SG onset (§1.6): the error is a systematic early bias (about -0.25 s for a braking step), not a symmetric '±0.25 s'. Apply it as a known bias correction, alongside the per-class dev bias.
- Minor inconsistency: §0.1 says red_light events 'last about 1–3 s', but §3's table gives 2–6 s. The spec's end, 'vehicle leaves the intersection', typically makes them longer than 3 s, which relaxes the IoU-0.7 tolerance the report cites for red_light.


## Missing points
- No plan to validate rules for classes absent from the samples. The spec says test videos 'contain events not in the samples', so wrong_way, U-turn, fire and obstacle may have no dev instance at all. Recommend synthetic trajectory tests (scripted tracks in scene BEV coordinates) per class to unit-test logic and boundaries, plus physically motivated fixed thresholds where dev tuning is impossible.
- No open-weights VLM or clip classifier used as a second-stage verifier for rare-class candidates (fire_smoke, road_obstacle, accident, wrong_way). The spec explicitly allows 'open VLMs such as Qwen-VL or InternVL', and verifying a handful of short candidate clips per video is cheap. This is the most direct route to the q ≈ 0 the gating rule needs.
- Macro weighting and priorities: every present class weighs 1/|C|, so a rare class with 1–2 GT instances is worth as much as stopped_vehicle. The implementation order ranks by ease and does not estimate the expected Score_A gain per class, which is p·F/|C|.
- Annotation-convention risk: per-class bias calibration learned from the team's own labels transfers the team's own conventions, not the organizers'. The report should say to inspect the starter-kit examples/ground_truth.json for boundary conventions and to keep bias corrections small and physically motivated.
- Determinism: the spec requires 'two runs on same machine must give same predictions.json up to float noise'. Hard thresholds amplify tiny nondeterminism in the GPU detector (cuDNN autotune, fp16) into appearing or vanishing events. The rule layer should use hysteresis and margins, and the pipeline should fix cudnn.deterministic, batch shapes and the decode path.
- Signal-state decoding practicalities: LED signal-head flicker or aliasing at 25 fps, exposure saturation at night, and lamp ROIs drifting due to slight camera shake. The '0.04 s transition accuracy' is an unmarked estimate that assumes lamp sampling at every frame.
- Event handling at video edges: a stopped_vehicle visible for less than 10 s at the start or end of a clip (label or not?), and how 'leaves the frame' is timed when §1.3 discards truncated boxes near the border (end times for red_light, wrong_way and accident rely on frame-exit time).
- No explicit end-to-end runtime budget: pass-1 detection at stride 1 or 2 at native resolution on a T4, plus B's own per-frame processing, plus any full-rate second passes and an in-A RiskEstimator run, measured against '3x video duration' with margin (Code rubric: 'stays inside time budget with margin').
- Evidence export for the website: the rule engine should save per-event evidence (actors, snapshots, key signals) to produce the required 'examples of each class detected, honest failure cases' and the class-confusion and error analysis extra credit.
- Organizer questions not asked: whether normal red-phase queues count as congestion, and whether vehicles stopped inside a non-signal jam count as stopped_vehicle. These directly determine two frequent classes.
